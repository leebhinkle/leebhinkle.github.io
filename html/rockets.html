Scrap Heap

I started work on Scrap Heap after launching several smaller diameter rockets out of sight. My goals were to build something larger that was light enough to fly on E motors and strong enough to handle higher powered motors. Starting with two damaged sections of 3" thin-wall tubing, construction was through-the-wall papered balsa fins along with multiple 3D printed centering rings that held a long section of 38mm thin wall tubing. The inner tube extended up the majority of the main body tube for strenght and to reduce the ejection charge volume. The nose cone was a new 3 piece fall away design that holds the parachute to protect it from the ejection charge and move the weight as far forward as possible. Scarp Heap flew well on a E12-4 and an F41W-5 twice. During the fourth flight the G40W-7 motor exploded shortly after clearing the launch rail and the rocket was destroyed. I'd have much rather found the limits of the design and construction (I think it would handle a longer burning H!) but I learned a lot and am moving on to other design approaches.

Easy A

I wanted to build something that would be cheap, easy to assemble, and could fly on 18mm A motors in a park or C motors with a larger field. Goals were similar to my Pfft rocket but after I lost one on a mini-motor I decided a bit larger rocket that was also easier to build would be better. The main part is a 3D printed motor mount, shock cord attach, launch lug, fin assembly that along with the retaining ring forms the entire bottom of the rocket once epoxied (yes use epoxy!) to the body tube. The nose cone is a two piece design that separates on ejection and holds/protects the streamer. First version used solid flat fins which broke on two of the first four flights. I updated the design to airfoiled infill fins and so far it has flown without issue. The slightly thicker fins reduced the altitude by a few percent.
